How to Collect Estate Dues, Association Levies and Union Fees Without Wahala

By DAPDEK Global Ventures · 27 November 2026

Ask any estate chairman, union leader or alumni treasurer what the hardest part of the job is, and many will say the same thing: collecting dues and proving where the money went. Members owe, some say they have paid, the old treasurer’s records are incomplete, and the AGM turns into a shouting match.

It does not have to be like this. A few good habits make collection smoother and accounts easy to defend.

Start with a clean member register

You cannot collect from people you have not listed. Keep one register with every member or household, their phone number and what they are expected to pay: monthly dues, special levies (for example a road or security levy) and any fines your rules allow.

Issue a receipt for every payment

Every naira collected should get a receipt with a number, the member’s name, what it was for and the date. Receipts protect the member and the treasurer. When a member later says "I paid in March", the receipt number ends the argument.

Know who is owing, and remind them early

A current debtors list is the most useful thing a treasurer can have. Send polite reminders with the exact amount owed. People pay faster when they know precisely what is outstanding and why.

Record spending as carefully as income

Members want to know what their money was used for. Record every expense: what, how much, when, approved by whom. Then income and expenditure reports are easy to produce for meetings.

Never delete a mistake

If a payment was entered wrongly, cancel it with a reason instead of erasing it. Records that can be quietly changed are records nobody trusts.

Plan the handover

Executives change. A clean handover report (members, balances, debtors, income and expenses) means the new treasurer starts from facts, not from a plastic bag of old receipts.

Tip: Read a short summary of income, spending and debtors at every general meeting. Regular openness prevents suspicion from building up.

Make paying easy

The easier it is to pay, the more people pay on time. Announce the amounts and due dates clearly at the start of the year, send a reminder a few days before each due date and accept payment the way most members prefer. Always confirm payments with a receipt the same day, so members never wonder whether their payment was recorded.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Collecting cash without writing a receipt "because it is a small amount".
  • Keeping association money in a personal account with personal spending.
  • Allowing one person to collect, record and spend without anyone else seeing the records.
  • Waiting until the end of the year to chase debtors, when the amounts have grown too large.
  • Starting a new executive with no handover, so old debts are forgotten.

In summary

Keep one member register, give receipts every time, chase debtors early and politely, record spending carefully, never delete mistakes and prepare a clean handover. Your members will trust the executive, and collection gets easier every year.
