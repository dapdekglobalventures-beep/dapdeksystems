Church Management Software: Keeping Members, Attendance and Giving Records Safe

By DAPDEK Global Ventures · 27 November 2026

Churches handle a lot of information: members and their families, new converts, workers and departments, attendance at services, tithes, offerings, special donations and spending. In many churches all of this lives in exercise books, loose sheets and the memory of one secretary.

When that secretary travels, or a book is lost, the church loses its records. Good records are part of good stewardship.

1. Know your members

A proper membership register with names, phone numbers, pictures and department makes pastoral care easier. You can follow up on members who have stopped coming, celebrate birthdays and plan for departments.

2. Track attendance

Attendance records show growth or decline over time and help leaders spot members who need a visit.

3. Record giving transparently

Tithes, offerings and donations should be counted by more than one person and recorded the same day. Clear records protect the people who handle church money and build trust in the congregation.

4. Budget and record spending

A simple budget for each department, with spending recorded against it, keeps the church’s finances healthy and makes reports to leaders easy.

5. Reports for leaders

Pastors and church councils need summaries: membership, attendance trends, income and expenditure. Records that can be summarised quickly lead to better decisions.

Tip: Agree a counting routine for offerings: at least two people count, both sign, and the figure is entered before anyone leaves.

6. Keep it working offline

The church office should not stop because data has finished. Software that works offline and online is ideal.

Following up new converts and visitors

Many churches welcome visitors every Sunday but lose touch with them by the following week. Recording each visitor’s name, phone number and who invited them, then assigning a follow-up worker, turns first-time visitors into members. A good record system shows at a glance who has been contacted and who has not.

Common record-keeping problems in churches

  • Membership lists that have not been updated in years.
  • Offering figures written on loose paper that goes missing.
  • Departments spending without a budget.
  • One person holding all records, with no copy anywhere else.
  • Reports prepared from memory before council meetings.

Each of these can be fixed with a clear routine and one place where records live, backed up so that losing one book or one computer never means losing the church’s history.

Getting started without stress

Moving from books to software does not have to happen in one weekend. Start with the membership register: enter current members department by department, with help from each department head. Next, begin recording attendance and giving from a chosen Sunday. Once leaders see the first monthly report, add budgets and spending. Train at least two people to use the system, so the records never depend on one person, and keep regular backups on a flash drive or your own cloud.
